# Computer with real numbers
## Computers 
### Numbers on a computer
**Integers**: 
- for any n bit number, $range = -2^{n-1} $ to $ 2^{n - 1} - 1$
- The standard is to use two's complement where negative numbers are obtained by inverting all bits of the positive counterpart then adding 1 
- leftmost bit acts like a **sign bit** with 0 being non-negative and 1 being negative 

*Example*: represent \(-5\) using 8 bits.

\[
5 = 00000101_2
\]

Flip the bits:

\[
11111010_2
\]

Add 1:

\[
11111011_2
\]

Therefore,

\[
-5 = 11111011_2.
\]

**Floating-point numbers (FPNs)**: approximate real numbers and are not evenly spaced.
FPNs are computer approximations to real numbers. A normalised binary FPN has the form, with \(d_j \in \{0,1\}\) for \(j = 1, \ldots, p\) for some \(p > 0\),

\[
\pm (1.d_1 d_2 \ldots d_p)_2 \times 2^e,
\qquad e_{\min} \leq e \leq e_{\max}.
\]

In double precision (54 bits):
- 1 bit for the sign 
- 52 bits for the fraction (mantissa) so p = 52
- 11 bits for the exponent field so $e_{min} = 0$ and $e_{max} = 2047$ 

*Characteristics of FPNs*
1 is represented in FPN as $1.000...0 * 2 ^0$. The next normalised number would be $1.000...1 * 2 ^0$. 
- The difference 1 and the next normalised FPN ($2^{-52}$ for doubles) is the **relative machine precision ($\epsilon$)**. 
 - Largest finite FPN = $(2 - \epsilon) \times 2^{1023}$  
  MATLAB `realmax`, Python `sys.float_info.max`

- Smallest positive *normalized* FPN = $2^{-1022}$  
  MATLAB `realmin`, Python `sys.float_info.min`

**Definition (Infinity and Not a Number)**

- **Overflow**: any number much larger than the largest finite FPN is represented by `inf`;  
  any number much smaller than the most negative finite FPN is represented by `-inf`.

- **Not a Number**: any result that is mathematically undefined is represented by `NaN`.
- **Underflow**: A number smaller than the smallest positive normalized FPN may be
represented as a subnormal number; if it is even smaller, it underflows to 0.

## Errors 
Let $\bar{x}$ be a computed approximation to $x$.

- The **absolute error** is  
  $$
  e_a = |\bar{x} - x|
  $$

- For $x \ne 0$, the **relative error** is  
  $$
  e_r = \frac{|\bar{x} - x|}{|x|}
  $$

- The **number of significant decimal figures** (nsf) is the largest positive integer $p$ such that  
  $$
  e_r < \frac{1}{2}10^{-p}
  $$

**Definition (Rounding error)**

The **rounding error** in storing $x$ on a computer is the absolute error between the exact value of $x$ and the stored value.

**Estimating rounding error**

- $\epsilon$ is the relative machine precision on a computer.

- $\epsilon |x|$ gives an estimate, or a bound, of the rounding error in storing $x$ on a computer.

**Definition (Catastrophic cancellation)**
If $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$ are computed approximations to nonzero quantities $x$ and $y$, with $x \approx y$, then the subtraction $\bar{x} - \bar{y}$ may lose many significant digits.

In particular, the relative error in $\bar{x} - \bar{y}$ can be much larger than the relative errors in $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$.

- Subtracting nearly equal quantities is dangerous because the leading digits cancel.

- This often produces a result with much lower accuracy than the original data.

## Efficiency - Time

**Definition (Floating-point operation)**
◊
A **flop** is one of the basic operations $+, -, *, /$ applied to FPNs.

- **flops** and **flops/sec** (floating-point operations per second) are used to estimate the time a task will take.

- Assume $+, -, *, /$ take the same time. In reality, $*$ and $/$ usually take longer than $+$ and $-$.

- **Performance units**
  - Mflops, Megaflops $= 10^6$ flops
  - Gflops, Gigaflops $= 10^9$ flops
  - Tflops, Teraflops $= 10^{12}$ flops

- **Speed**:
  $ \text{flops/sec} = \text{Clock cycles/sec}  \times \text{Cores}  \times \text{flops/cycle}  $

  - Clock rate: in GHz, that is, $10^9$ cycles per second
  - Cores: CPUs with 4--16 cores, GPUs with thousands of cores, or Many Integrated Core (MIC) coprocessors with 50--80 cores
  - flops/cycle: modern CPUs can do 4 or more
